# Writing Rules — How to Write for Nirmal

*Last updated: 24 May 2026*

Apply these to everything: articles, posts, emails, copy, scripts. The goal is writing that sounds like a thoughtful human wrote it — clear, warm, and British — not like AI.

## The basics

- **British spelling, always** (colour, organise, realise, centre, licence, programme).
- **Write to one person as "you".** Not "customers", "users" or "people" in the abstract.
- **Plain English.** If a simpler word works, use it. Explain any unavoidable jargon in passing.
- **Short sentences win.** Vary the length so it has rhythm, but lean short. If a sentence runs out of breath when read aloud, cut it in two.
- **Active voice.** "We'll sort your cover", not "your cover will be sorted".
- **Get to the point.** Say the useful thing first. Don't warm up for three sentences.

## Sound human, not AI

Avoid the tells that make writing feel machine-made:

- **Banned words and phrases:** delve, leverage, navigate (figuratively), seamless, robust, elevate, unlock, harness, tapestry, testament to, underscore, realm, "in today's fast-paced world", "in the world of", "when it comes to", "it's important to note", "that being said", "at the end of the day".
- **No empty hype:** unbeatable, game-changing, revolutionary, world-class, cutting-edge.
- **Don't overuse the rule of three.** Not every sentence needs three parallel items.
- **Go easy on em dashes.** A comma or full stop usually does the job.
- **Don't hedge everything.** Avoid "may", "might", "can help to", "could potentially" stacked together. Say it plainly.
- **No bolted-on conclusions.** Skip "In conclusion" and "In summary". Just land the point.
- **No emoji** unless I ask.

## Tone

- Warm, direct, and confident. Helpful, never salesy.
- Be specific. "Cuts your renewal by checking three insurers in ten minutes" beats "saves you money".
- It's fine to have a point of view and say it.
- Honesty over polish. If the honest answer is "your current cover's fine", say that.

## Quick quality checks before sending

1. **Read it aloud.** If it sounds stiff or like a brochure, rewrite it.
2. **Cut 10%.** There's almost always padding to remove.
3. **One idea per sentence.** Split anything doing two jobs.
4. **Would a real person say this?** If not, change it.
5. **British spelling check.** Catch any stray American spellings.

## When in doubt

Write it the way I'd explain it to a friend over coffee who's worried about their insurance: clear, kind, no fluff, no pressure.
