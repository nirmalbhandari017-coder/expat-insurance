# Brand Voice — Expat Protect Hub

*Last updated: 24 May 2026*

How Expat Protect Hub sounds in everything it writes: site copy, articles, emails, social posts, replies. Insurance is a stressful, confusing topic for most people. Our job is to make it feel calmer and clearer — and to sound like a real, knowledgeable human while we do it.

## The voice in a sentence

A warm, expert friend who happens to know expat insurance inside out — reassuring, straight-talking, and never salesy.

## Three traits that define us

**Warm and reassuring.** We take the worry out of a stressful topic. Calm, kind, on the reader's side. We acknowledge that this stuff is confusing before we explain it.

**Expert and trustworthy.** We know what we're talking about and it shows. We're accurate, specific, and honest — including when the honest answer isn't the most expensive option.

**Friendly and conversational.** We sound like a person, not a policy document. We use plain words, write the way people speak, and address the reader directly as "you".

## How formal?

**Polished and professional — but human.** Credible and well-written, never stiff or corporate. Think a trusted adviser writing a clear, friendly email, not an insurer's terms and conditions. Contractions are fine ("you'll", "we're"). Jargon is not.

## Never sound like

**Salesy or pushy.** No hard sell, no pressure, no "act now or miss out", no too-good-to-be-true promises. We help people decide; we don't corner them.

**Cold or corporate.** No insurer-speak, no faceless jargon, no hiding behind formality. If a real person wouldn't say it out loud, we don't write it.

## Do / Don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Write to one person as "you" | Address "customers" or "clients" in the abstract |
| Use plain English | Use jargon like "indemnity", "underwriting", "exclusions" without explaining them |
| Lead with what helps the reader | Lead with what sells a policy |
| Be specific and honest | Make vague or absolute promises |
| Acknowledge the confusion, then clear it up | Assume everyone already understands insurance |
| Keep sentences short and readable | Pile up clauses and qualifiers |

## Words and phrases

**Lean into:** peace of mind, properly covered, right cover for you, real person, honest, plain English, no pressure, here to help, what this actually means.

**Avoid:** unbeatable, cheapest, guaranteed, hassle-free miracle claims, "in today's world", "navigate", "leverage", "seamless", "robust", buzzwords and hype generally.

## A quick before / after

> **Too corporate:** "Our comprehensive solutions empower expatriates to optimise their international healthcare coverage."
>
> **On brand:** "Living abroad shouldn't mean guessing whether you're covered. We'll help you find a plan that genuinely fits your life — and a real person to talk it through with."

> **Too salesy:** "Don't miss out — get the cheapest expat insurance today before prices rise!"
>
> **On brand:** "If your premium's just gone up, it's worth a second look. We'll tell you honestly whether there's a better fit out there."

## One compliance note

Insurance copy can't over-promise. Avoid absolute guarantees about cover, claims or outcomes ("you'll always be covered", "guaranteed payout"). Keep claims accurate and honest — it also happens to be exactly how we want to sound anyway.
